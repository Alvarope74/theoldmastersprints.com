Impressionism at home: 6 paintings by Monet, Renoir, Seurat and Manet to decorate with

Impressionism is the movement that, from the 1870s, took painting outdoors to capture light and the passing moment with loose brushstrokes and bright colours. It is also one of the easiest styles to live with: luminous, cheerful and never heavy.

Where the name comes from

The name comes from a painting: Claude Monet’s Impression, Sunrise (1872). At the 1874 exhibition in Paris, the critic Louis Leroy used the title to mock the group as “the Impressionists”, and they ended up adopting the label.

For a bright living room

Renoir’s Bal du moulin de la Galette (1876) is pure summer light through the trees: it looks great in landscape above the sofa, in A2 or A1. Seurat’s A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte, painted with thousands of dots of colour, is wide and calm and rewards a closer look.

For a corner with character

Impression, Sunrise is small and almost abstract: an orange sun over a blue harbour. It works in A3 in a hallway or bedroom. A Bar at the Folies-Bergère, Manet’s last major work (1882), plays with a mirror that is still debated today: a great conversation piece for the dining room.

The forerunner: Manet

Manet never exhibited with the Impressionists, but Luncheon on the Grass (1863) opened the way by painting a modern scene without the academy’s rules. If you like the style but want something bolder, it is a good alternative.

How to style them

The Impressionists use soft blues, greens and ochres, so they pair well with white or sand-coloured walls, light wood and linen. A thin natural-wood or white frame respects their light better than a heavy gilt one. And if you want to step up to intense colour, Van Gogh’s The Starry Night (Post-Impressionism) is the next step.

Frequently asked questions

Why is it called Impressionism?
After Monet’s painting Impression, Sunrise (1872). In 1874 a critic used the title to mock the group and the name stuck.
Which Impressionist painting works above a sofa?
A bright landscape-format one, such as Renoir’s Bal du moulin de la Galette or Seurat’s A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte, in A2 or A1.
Where can you see the most famous Impressionist paintings?
Above all at the Musée d’Orsay in Paris, which holds the world’s largest Impressionist collection, and at the Musée Marmottan Monet.
