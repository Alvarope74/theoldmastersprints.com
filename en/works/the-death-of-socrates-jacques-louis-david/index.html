The Death of Socrates by Jacques-Louis David: meaning, location and key facts

The Death of Socrates is an oil on canvas by Jacques-Louis David, painted in 1787. It measures 129.5 × 196.2 cm and is held at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York.

What it shows

Socrates, sentenced to death in 399 BC for impiety and corrupting the young, calmly reaches for the cup of hemlock while still talking about the immortality of the soul. With his other hand he points upwards. His disciples weep around him.

At the foot of the bed sits Plato, turned away, and Crito touches his master’s leg. David followed Plato’s account in the Phaedo and turned it into a manifesto of civic virtue: reason and duty above fear.

Did you know?

  • Plato is shown as an old man, although he was about 28 when Socrates died.
  • It was shown at the Paris Salon of 1787 and was one of the great successes of Neoclassicism before the Revolution.
  • The Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York acquired it in 1931 through the Catharine Lorillard Wolfe Collection fund.
