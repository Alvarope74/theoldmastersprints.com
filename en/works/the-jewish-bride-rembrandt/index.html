The Jewish Bride by Rembrandt: meaning, location and key facts

The Jewish Bride is an oil on canvas by Rembrandt, painted between about 1665 and 1669. It measures 121.5 × 166.5 cm and is held at the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam.

StyleDutch Golden Age
Data sourceWikidata (Q766782) · Wikipedia

What it shows

A couple embraces tenderly: he rests his hand on her breast and she covers it with hers. Both wear rich clothes of red and gold. Nobody knows who they are; one theory is that they represent Isaac and Rebecca from the Old Testament.

Rembrandt laid the paint on very thickly on the man’s golden sleeve, sometimes with a palette knife, building up relief. The title The Jewish Bride was given by a collector in the 19th century and has no documentary basis.

Did you know?

  • Vincent van Gogh wrote that he would give ten years of his life to sit in front of this painting for two weeks with only a crust of bread.
  • It was painted between 1665 and 1669, in Rembrandt’s last years.
  • It hangs in the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam.
