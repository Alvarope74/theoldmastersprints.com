Whistler's Mother by James McNeill Whistler: meaning, size and where to see it

Whistler's Mother is an oil on canvas by James McNeill Whistler, painted in 1871. It measures 144.3 × 163 cm and is held at the Musée d'Orsay, Paris.

What it shows

Anna McNeill Whistler, the painter’s mother, sits in profile on a chair in a long black dress and a white lace cap, her hands folded in her lap over a handkerchief. Her feet rest on a footstool. On the left hangs a dark curtain with a floral pattern, and on the grey wall is a framed print: Black Lion Wharf (1859), a view of the Thames by Whistler himself.

Whistler painted it in 1871 in his studio in Chelsea, London. Its title is Arrangement in Grey and Black No. 1: what mattered to him was the harmony of tones and shapes, not likeness or sentiment, and he complained that the public cared who the sitter was. Even so, the still figure and the almost monochrome palette turned the picture into a universal image of motherhood and one of the most famous works by an American painter.

Did you know?

  • The French state bought it in 1891 for the Musée du Luxembourg; it later moved to the Louvre and, in 1986, to the Musée d’Orsay.
  • In 1934 the United States Post Office issued a three-cent stamp showing the painting to mark Mother’s Day.
  • It is the central painting of the film Bean (1997), in which Mr Bean escorts it to a museum in Los Angeles and accidentally damages it.

Where to see the original

It is in the Musée d’Orsay in Paris, on the Left Bank of the Seine. It measures 144.3 × 163 cm. It is one of the few paintings by an American artist in the museum’s collection, and it often travels on loan to exhibitions in the United States.
