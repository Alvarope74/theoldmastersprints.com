Luncheon of the Boating Party by Pierre-Auguste Renoir: meaning, size and where to see it

Luncheon of the Boating Party is an oil on canvas by Pierre-Auguste Renoir, painted between 1880 and 1881. It measures 130.2 × 175.6 cm and is held at the Phillips Collection, Washington.

What it shows

A group of Renoir’s friends finish lunch on the balcony of the Maison Fournaise restaurant, on the island of Chatou on the Seine, under a red and white striped awning. Bottles, glasses and fruit are left on the table; behind them, through the trees, the sails of boats on the river can be seen. In the left foreground the seamstress Aline Charigot, who would later become his wife, plays with a little dog; on the right, sitting astride a chair in a rower’s vest and straw boater, is the painter Gustave Caillebotte.

Leaning on the railing are Alphonse Fournaise, the owner’s son, and his sister Alphonsine; in the background, in a top hat, is the collector Charles Ephrussi, and in the centre, drinking from a glass, the actress Ellen Andrée. Renoir painted it in 1880–1881 with fourteen figures chatting, drinking and flirting, combining a group portrait, a still life and a landscape in a single picture, with the light of a summer afternoon filtering through the awning.

Did you know?

  • It was shown at the seventh Impressionist exhibition in Paris in 1882; it had been bought by the dealer Paul Durand-Ruel, the great champion of the Impressionists.
  • The American collector Duncan Phillips bought it from the Durand-Ruel family in 1923 for $125,000, and it has been the most famous work in his museum ever since.
  • Ellen Andrée, the woman drinking in the centre, is the same actress who had posed for Degas’s L’Absinthe a few years earlier.

Where to see the original

It is in the Phillips Collection in Washington, D.C., in the Dupont Circle neighbourhood, which describes itself as America’s first museum of modern art. It measures 130.2 × 175.6 cm. The Maison Fournaise at Chatou is still open as a restaurant and museum beside the Seine, west of Paris.
