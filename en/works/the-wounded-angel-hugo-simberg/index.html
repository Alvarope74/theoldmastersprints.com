The Wounded Angel by Hugo Simberg: meaning, size and where to see it

The Wounded Angel is an oil on canvas by Hugo Simberg, painted in 1903. It measures 127 × 154 cm and is held at the Ateneum, Helsinki.

What it shows

Two boys dressed in dark clothes carry a wounded angel on a stretcher: a white-winged girl with a bandaged head and a bloodstained wing, who lowers her head and clutches a small bunch of snowdrops, flowers that symbolise healing and rebirth. The boy in front looks at the ground; the one behind, in a black hat, turns towards the viewer with a serious expression.

The landscape is real: the path through Eläintarha park in Helsinki, with Töölönlahti Bay in the background, a leisure spot for the working classes where several charitable institutions stood at the time; the boys are carrying the angel towards the Blind Girls’ School and the Home for Cripples. Simberg refused to explain the picture, preferring viewers to draw their own conclusions; he painted it in 1903, after spending much of the previous winter in bed with meningitis.

Did you know?

  • In 2006 it was voted Finland’s “national painting” in a poll organised by the Ateneum art museum.
  • It was shown at the Ateneum autumn exhibition of 1903 and thrilled his teacher Akseli Gallen-Kallela; Simberg wrote to his sister Blenda that he had achieved “something of a grand succès” among his colleagues and the jury.
  • It was his favourite painting, and in 1905–1906 he painted a larger version among his frescoes for St John’s Church in Tampere, now the cathedral. It inspired an album cover by Dimmu Borgir and the Nightwish music video “Amaranth”.

Where to see the original

It is in the Ateneum Art Museum in Helsinki, part of the Finnish National Gallery, opposite the central railway station. It measures 127 × 154 cm. The path along Töölönlahti Bay, the same one as in the painting, is still there.
