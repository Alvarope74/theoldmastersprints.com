The Third of May 1808 by Francisco Goya: meaning, location and key facts

The Third of May 1808 is an oil on canvas by Francisco Goya, painted in 1814. It measures 268 × 347 cm and is held at the Museo del Prado, Madrid.

Data sourceWikidata (Q1091086) · Wikipedia

What it shows

It recreates the execution of Madrid civilians by Napoleon’s troops in the early hours of 3 May 1808, after the uprising of the previous day. A man in a white shirt flings his arms open before the firing squad, lit by a lantern on the ground, while others wait their turn or lie dead.

The soldiers are seen from behind and without faces, like a machine. Goya painted it in 1814, together with The Second of May 1808, after offering the Regency Council to “perpetuate with the brush the most notable and heroic actions” against Napoleon.

Did you know?

  • The palms of the man in white bear marks like stigmata, likening him to Christ.
  • It directly inspired Manet’s The Execution of Emperor Maximilian and Picasso’s Massacre in Korea.
  • It was damaged in an accident involving the lorry evacuating it during the Spanish Civil War, and was restored.

Where to see the original

The Third of May 1808 is at the Museo del Prado, Madrid. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
