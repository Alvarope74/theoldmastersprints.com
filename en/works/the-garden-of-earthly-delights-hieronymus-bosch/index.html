The Garden of Earthly Delights by Hieronymus Bosch: meaning, location and key facts

The Garden of Earthly Delights is an oil on panel by Hieronymus Bosch, painted between about 1490 and 1510. It measures 205.5 × 390 cm and is held at the Museo del Prado, Madrid.

MuseumMuseo del Prado, Madrid
StyleEarly Netherlandish painting
Data sourceWikidata (Q321303) · Wikipedia

What it shows

It is a triptych. The left panel shows Paradise, with God presenting Eve to Adam. The central panel, a vast garden full of naked figures, giant fruit, birds and fantastic fountains, is usually read as humanity given over to pleasure. The right panel is a hell of musical instruments, fire and punishments.

When closed, the shutters show the creation of the world in grisaille, as a transparent sphere. Bosch painted it around 1490-1510 and its exact meaning is still debated; the moralising reading is the most widely accepted.

Did you know?

  • Philip II of Spain acquired it and in 1593 sent it to El Escorial, where inventories described it as “the variety of the world”.
  • In the hell panel there is a musical score painted on a sinner’s buttocks; in 2014 it was transcribed and recorded.
  • The title “Garden of Earthly Delights” is modern and does not appear in early documents.

Where to see the original

The Garden of Earthly Delights is at the Museo del Prado, Madrid. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
