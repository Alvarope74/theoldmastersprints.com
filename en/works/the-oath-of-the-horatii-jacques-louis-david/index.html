The Oath of the Horatii by Jacques-Louis David: meaning, location and key facts

The Oath of the Horatii is an oil on canvas by Jacques-Louis David, painted in 1784. It measures 330 × 425 cm and is held at the Louvre, Paris.

Date1784

StyleNeoclassicism
Data sourceWikidata (Q476458) · Wikipedia

What it shows

Three Roman brothers, the Horatii, swear before their father that they will fight to the death against the three Curiatii, champions of the city of Alba Longa, to settle the war between the two cities. The father raises their swords to the sky.

On the right the women collapse in grief: one of them, Camilla, is engaged to one of the Curiatii. The scene is organised under three arches, with straight lines and tense gestures set against the curves of the female figures.

Did you know?

  • It was commissioned by Louis XVI’s administration, and David painted it in Rome, where it was shown in his studio before reaching the Salon of 1785.
  • David made it larger than agreed, at 3.30 × 4.25 metres.
  • It is considered the painting that defines French Neoclassicism.

Where to see the original
