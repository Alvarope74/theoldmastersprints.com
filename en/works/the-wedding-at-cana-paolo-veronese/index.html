The Wedding at Cana by Paolo Veronese: meaning, location and key facts

The Wedding at Cana is an oil on canvas by Paolo Veronese, painted in 1563. It measures 677 × 994 cm and is held at the Louvre, Paris.

Date1563

MuseumLouvre, Paris
StyleVenetian School
Data sourceWikidata (Q185255) · Wikipedia

What it shows

Jesus, his mother and his disciples attend a wedding at Cana, where he turns water into wine, his first miracle according to the Gospel of John. Veronese sets it as a 16th-century Venetian banquet with more than a hundred figures, classical architecture and sumptuous clothing.

In the centre a group of musicians plays for the guests; according to tradition, Veronese portrayed himself among them with Titian, Tintoretto and Jacopo Bassano.

Did you know?

  • It was painted in 1562-1563 for the refectory of the monastery of San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice.
  • Napoleon’s troops took it to Paris in 1797, cut into pieces; it was not returned in 1815 but exchanged for a painting by Le Brun.
  • At 6.77 × 9.94 metres, it is the largest painting in the Louvre.
