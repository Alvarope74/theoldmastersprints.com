Famous paintings at the Louvre: 8 masterpieces to see

The Louvre opened as a museum in 1793 in the former royal palace in Paris and is the most visited museum in the world. It holds European painting from the Middle Ages to the mid-19th century, and its busiest room is the one with the Mona Lisa.

Must-see works

Mona Lisa

Leonardo da Vinci · c. 1503–1519

It is the portrait of Lisa Gherardini, wife of the Florentine merchant Francesco del Giocondo, which explains both of its names: La Gioconda and Mona Lisa (“mona” is short for madonna, “my lady”).

Liberty Leading the People

Eugène Delacroix · 1830

A woman in a Phrygian cap, the allegory of Liberty, strides over a barricade holding the French tricolour in one hand and a bayonetted musket in the other.

The Raft of the Medusa

Théodore Géricault · 1818–1819

In July 1816 the French frigate Méduse ran aground off the coast of Mauritania.

The Oath of the Horatii

Jacques-Louis David · 1784

Three Roman brothers, the Horatii, swear before their father that they will fight to the death against the three Curiatii, champions of the city of Alba Longa, to settle the war between the two cities.

The Virgin and Child with Saint Anne

Leonardo da Vinci · c. 1503–1519

The Virgin Mary, seated on the lap of her mother, Saint Anne, leans forward to hold the Christ Child, who plays with a lamb, a symbol of his future sacrifice.

The Coronation of Napoleon

Jacques-Louis David · 1805–1807

The coronation ceremony of Napoleon in Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris on 2 December 1804.

Grande Odalisque

Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres · 1814

An odalisque, a woman of an imagined harem, lies naked with her back to us on a divan and turns her face towards the viewer.

The Wedding at Cana

Paolo Veronese · 1563

Jesus, his mother and his disciples attend a wedding at Cana, where he turns water into wine, his first miracle according to the Gospel of John.
