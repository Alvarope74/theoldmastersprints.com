American Gothic by Grant Wood: meaning, location and key facts

American Gothic is an oil on beaverboard by Grant Wood, painted in 1930. It measures 78 × 65.3 cm and is held at the Art Institute of Chicago.

Data sourceWikidata (Q464782) · Wikipedia

What it shows

A stern farmer holding a pitchfork and a younger woman stand in front of a white wooden house with a Gothic Revival window. Grant Wood said they were a father and his daughter, although they are often read as husband and wife.

The models were the artist’s sister, Nan Wood Graham, and his dentist, Byron McKeeby. The house exists and stands in Eldon, Iowa. The vertical lines of the pitchfork are echoed in the window, the man’s shirt and the building.

Did you know?

  • In 1930 it won a bronze medal and 300 dollars in a competition at the Art Institute of Chicago, which bought it.
  • The Eldon house in the background is now a small museum, the American Gothic House Center.
  • It is one of the most parodied images in American art, in advertising, film and magazine covers.

Where to see the original

American Gothic is at the Art Institute of Chicago. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
