Morning in a Pine Forest by Ivan Shishkin: meaning, size and where to see it

Morning in a Pine Forest is an oil on canvas by Ivan Shishkin, painted in 1889. It measures 139 × 213 cm and is held at the Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow.

What it shows

Dawn breaks over a pine forest wrapped in mist. In the foreground, a great pine torn up by the roots and snapped in two lies on the ground, and four bears play on its branches and broken trunk: a mother keeps watch while three cubs climb, peer out and look into the forest. In the background, tall straight trunks fade into the haze, and the first sunlight gilds the treetops.

At first the painting had no bears. It was Konstantin Savitsky who suggested adding them, and Shishkin, who doubted his own skill as an animal painter, asked him to paint them: a wild family playing among the roots and trunks of the fallen pines. Shishkin is believed to have painted the pines from the forests of Gorodomlya Island on Lake Seliger, then untouched wilderness.

Did you know?

  • The canvas bore both signatures, “I. I. Shishkin” and “K. A. Savitsky”, but Pavel Tretyakov, who bought it soon after it was finished, had Savitsky’s removed with turpentine, arguing that from idea to execution everything was characteristic of Shishkin. That is why the painting is often credited to him alone.
  • The Einem confectionery factory, later Red October, agreed with the painting’s owner to copy it for a chocolate: the first “Mishka Kosolapy” (Clumsy Bear) sweets appeared in the early 1890s and won the Grand Prix at the 1900 Paris Exposition. In 1913 the artist Manuil Andreev put the scene on the wrapper, framed by spruce branches and Stars of Bethlehem.
  • It was formerly called The Bear Family in the Forest. Scholars later found that the preparatory drawings of the pine forest were made by both painters, not by Shishkin alone.

Where to see the original

It is in the Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow, the museum founded by Pavel Tretyakov himself. It measures 139 × 213 cm.
