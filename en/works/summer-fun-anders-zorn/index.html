Summer Fun by Anders Zorn: meaning, location and key facts

Summer Fun is a watercolour on paper by Anders Zorn, painted in 1886. It measures 76 × 54 cm and is held in a private collection.

What it shows

A man in a straw hat brings a rowing boat alongside a jetty where a young woman in white is waiting. Behind them, the silver-grey water of the Stockholm archipelago stretches to low islands on the horizon.

The water is the extraordinary part: Zorn builds it from transparent layers of watercolour, letting reflections, the light of the sky and the green depths show beneath the surface. He painted it at Dalarö, a coastal village in the archipelago, in the summer of 1886.

Did you know?

  • In 2010 it sold at auction in Stockholm for 26 million kronor, about €2.9 million, becoming the most expensive Swedish painting sold up to that time.
  • Before 1887 Zorn worked almost entirely in watercolour; he then turned to oil, the medium of his portraits and his famous scenes of Dalarna.
  • It is a large watercolour for the medium: 76 × 54 cm.

Where to see the original

It is in a private collection, so it cannot be seen in any museum. For Anders Zorn’s work, the best collections are the Zorn Museum (Zornmuseet) in Mora and the Nationalmuseum in Stockholm.
