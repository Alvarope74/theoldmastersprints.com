Netherlandish Proverbs by Pieter Brueghel the Elder: meaning, location and key facts

Netherlandish Proverbs is an oil on oak panel by Pieter Brueghel the Elder, painted in 1559. It measures 117 × 163 cm and is held at the Gemäldegalerie, Berlin.

Data sourceWikidata (Q533619) · Wikipedia

What it shows

A Flemish village full of people doing absurd things: a woman puts a blue cloak on her husband (she is cheating on him), a man bangs his head against a wall, another casts roses before swine and someone fills in the well after the calf has drowned.

Each scene illustrates a Netherlandish proverb of the time; more than a hundred have been identified. It is a catalogue of human folly and at the same time a game for the viewer to discover the sayings one by one.

Did you know?

  • In its day it was also known as The Blue Cloak or The Topsy-Turvy World.
  • It was painted by Pieter Bruegel the Elder in 1559.
  • It has been in the Gemäldegalerie in Berlin since 1914.
