The Virgin and Child with Saint Anne by Leonardo da Vinci: meaning, location and key facts

The Virgin and Child with Saint Anne is an oil on panel by Leonardo da Vinci, painted between about 1503 and 1519. It measures 168 × 130 cm and is held at the Louvre, Paris.

Key facts

ArtistLeonardo da Vinci
Datec. 1503–1519
MediumOil on panel
Dimensions168 × 130 cm (height × width)
MuseumLouvre, Paris
StyleRenaissance
Data sourceWikidata (Q563727) · Wikipedia

What it shows

The Virgin Mary, seated on the lap of her mother, Saint Anne, leans forward to hold the Christ Child, who plays with a lamb, a symbol of his future sacrifice. The three figures form a gentle pyramid against a landscape of bluish mountains.

Leonardo worked on it for years, roughly between 1503 and 1519, and left it unfinished. The transitions of light and the haze of the background are among the most delicate in his work.

Did you know?

  • The Louvre’s 2011-2012 restoration was controversial: two members of the advisory committee resigned over the cleaning.
  • The Burlington House Cartoon, in the National Gallery in London, treats the same subject with a different composition.
  • Like the Mona Lisa, Leonardo kept it with him in France until his death.

Where to see the original

The Virgin and Child with Saint Anne is at the Louvre, Paris. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
