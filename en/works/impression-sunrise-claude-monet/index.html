Impression, Sunrise by Claude Monet: meaning, location and key facts

Impression, Sunrise is an oil on canvas by Claude Monet, painted in 1872. It measures 48 × 63 cm and is held at the Musée Marmottan Monet, Paris.

Data sourceWikidata (Q328523) · Wikipedia

What it shows

The port of Le Havre at dawn, wrapped in blue and grey mist. An orange sun rises over the water and casts a reflection of short brushstrokes; in the foreground two rowing boats are little more than dark patches, and cranes, masts and chimneys can just be made out behind.

Monet does not describe the harbour in detail: in a few quick strokes he paints the impression of a moment, the light and atmosphere of the morning. He painted it from a hotel window overlooking the port of the town where he grew up.

Did you know?

  • It was shown in 1874 at the group's first exhibition, in the photographer Nadar's former studio in Paris. The critic Louis Leroy mocked the title in Le Charivari by calling the painters “Impressionists”, and the name stuck.
  • In 1985 it was stolen from the Musée Marmottan together with eight other works; it was recovered in Corsica in 1990.
  • It is a small painting for its fame: 48 × 63 cm.

Where to see the original

It hangs in the Musée Marmottan Monet in Paris's 16th arrondissement, which holds the world's largest collection of works by Claude Monet.
