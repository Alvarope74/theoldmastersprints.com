Luncheon on the Grass by Édouard Manet: meaning, location and key facts

Luncheon on the Grass is an oil on canvas by Édouard Manet, painted in 1863. It measures 208 × 264.5 cm and is held at the Musée d'Orsay, Paris.

StyleImpressionism
Data sourceWikidata (Q152509) · Wikipedia

What it shows

Two fashionably dressed men talk in a wood next to a naked woman who looks out at the viewer; in the background another woman bathes in a stream. In front lie an overturned basket of fruit and bread and her discarded clothes.

Manet reworked classical models, an engraving by Marcantonio Raimondi after Raphael and the Pastoral Concert in the Louvre, but with real people of his own time. That, and the direct gaze of the model, Victorine Meurent, caused the scandal.

Did you know?

  • It was rejected by the official Salon of 1863 and shown at the Salon des Refusés created that year by order of Napoleon III.
  • Manet exhibited it under the title The Bath.
  • Monet painted his own Luncheon on the Grass in 1865-1866 as a response.

Where to see the original

Luncheon on the Grass is at the Musée d'Orsay, Paris. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
