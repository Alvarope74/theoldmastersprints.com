Giorgione: biography, paintings and prints

Castelfranco Véneto, h. 1477 – Venecia, 1510

Giorgio Barbarelli da Castelfranco, known as Giorgione, Italian painter of the Venetian school during the High Renaissance. According to Vasari he trained in Venice under Giovanni Bellini. With his younger colleague Titian he founded 16th-century Venetian painting, built on colour and mood rather than drawing, and in 1508 the two painted the frescoes on the facade of the Fondaco dei Tedeschi. He mostly painted small pictures for private collectors, with poetic subjects that are hard to decipher, such as The Tempest. He died of the plague in Venice in 1510, in his early thirties; only about six works are firmly attributed to him, and Titian finished some of them, such as the Sleeping Venus in Dresden.
