Wheatfield with Crows by Vincent van Gogh: meaning, location and key facts

Wheatfield with Crows is an oil on canvas by Vincent van Gogh, painted in 1890. It measures 50.2 × 103 cm and is held at the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam.

What it shows

A golden wheat field stretches under a stormy dark blue sky, crossed by a flock of black crows. Three paths open into the field without leading anywhere visible.

Van Gogh painted it in July 1890 in Auvers-sur-Oise, a few weeks before his death, in an elongated double-square format. In a letter he spoke of painting wheat fields under troubled skies to express sadness and extreme loneliness, but also what he found healthy in the countryside.

Did you know?

  • It is often said to be his last painting, but historians believe he painted others afterwards, such as Tree Roots.
  • It measures 50.2 × 103 cm, almost twice as wide as it is tall.
  • It belongs to the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam, which holds the largest collection of his work.

Where to see the original

Wheatfield with Crows is at the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
