The Hunters in the Snow by Pieter Brueghel the Elder: meaning, location and key facts

The Hunters in the Snow is an oil on panel by Pieter Brueghel the Elder, painted in 1565. It measures 117 × 162 cm and is held at the Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna.

Data sourceWikidata (Q500985) · Wikipedia

What it shows

Three hunters return to the village with their dogs after a poor day: all they carry is a fox. They pass an inn whose sign hangs askew while villagers tend a fire. Below, the frozen valley fills with skaters and tiny figures.

Bruegel painted it in 1565 as part of a series on the months or seasons for the merchant Niclaes Jongelinck in Antwerp. Five of the six panels survive.

Did you know?

  • The Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna holds three of the five surviving panels.
  • It appears in Andrei Tarkovsky’s Solaris (1972) and Lars von Trier’s Melancholia (2011).
  • The black birds on the branches and the greenish sky heighten the sense of cold.

Where to see the original

The Hunters in the Snow is at the Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
