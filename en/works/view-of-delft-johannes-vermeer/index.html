View of Delft by Johannes Vermeer: meaning, location and key facts

View of Delft is an oil on canvas by Johannes Vermeer, painted between about 1660 and 1661. It measures 96.5 × 115.7 cm and is held at the Mauritshuis, The Hague.

Key facts

MuseumMauritshuis, The Hague

Data sourceWikidata (Q523974) · Wikipedia

What it shows

Delft seen from the south, across the harbour on the river Schie, with the Schiedam and Rotterdam gates, the rooftops and the sunlit tower of the New Church. In the foreground, a few figures on the bank and moored barges.

More than half of the canvas is sky, with clouds casting shadow over the town. Vermeer used thick texture and small touches of light for the brickwork and the water, giving the feeling of a quiet, damp morning.

Did you know?

  • Marcel Proust called it “the most beautiful painting in the world”, and in his novel The Captive the writer Bergotte dies in front of its “little patch of yellow wall”.
  • The Dutch state bought it in 1822 for the Mauritshuis.
  • It is one of only two known townscapes by Vermeer; the other is The Little Street, in the Rijksmuseum.
