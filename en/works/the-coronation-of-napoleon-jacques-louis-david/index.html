The Coronation of Napoleon by Jacques-Louis David: meaning, location and key facts

The Coronation of Napoleon is an oil on canvas by Jacques-Louis David, painted between 1805 and 1807. It measures 621 × 979 cm and is held at the Louvre, Paris.

Key facts

ArtistJacques-Louis David
Date1805–1807
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions621 × 979 cm (height × width)
MuseumLouvre, Paris
StyleNeoclassicism
Data sourceWikidata (Q1231009) · Wikipedia

What it shows

The coronation ceremony of Napoleon in Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris on 2 December 1804. David chooses the moment when Napoleon, having crowned himself, is about to crown his wife Joséphine, who kneels before him. Pope Pius VII, seated, blesses the scene.

More than a hundred figures fill a canvas 6.21 × 9.79 metres wide. David attended the ceremony and portrayed the members of the court faithfully, although he changed details for political reasons.

Did you know?

  • Letizia Bonaparte, the emperor’s mother, appears in a gallery although she did not attend the ceremony.
  • David painted a second version, now at Versailles, in which one of Napoleon’s sisters wears pink.
  • It is one of the largest paintings in the Louvre.

Where to see the original

The Coronation of Napoleon is at the Louvre, Paris. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
