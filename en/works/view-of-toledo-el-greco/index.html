View of Toledo by El Greco: meaning, size and where to see it

View of Toledo is an oil on canvas by El Greco, painted between about 1596 and 1600. It measures 121.3 × 108.6 cm and is held at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York.

What it shows

Toledo under a stormy sky, with dark clouds torn by patches of white light. The city spreads along the top of a hill; on the right stand the Alcázar and the cathedral tower, and on the left, on another rise, the Castle of San Servando. Below, the River Tagus runs through its gorge, crossed by the Alcántara Bridge and its tower. Small figures fish or wash clothes by the water, a horseman wades across the river, and a road climbs between intensely green hills.

It is not an exact view. El Greco moved some buildings: the Alcázar and the cathedral appear with their relative positions reversed, although the Castle of San Servando is where it should be. For that reason it has been read as an ideal Toledo, a “city of the spirit”, rather than a topographical portrait. Landscape was rarely painted for its own sake in Spain at the time, and this canvas is often considered the one that began the genre there.

Did you know?

  • It is one of only two surviving landscapes by El Greco; the other, View and Plan of Toledo, was painted later and hangs in the El Greco Museum in Toledo. Both were in the collection of Pedro Salazar de Mendoza at his death, and he is believed to have commissioned them.
  • It is signed at lower right with his name in Greek letters, Doménikos Theotokópoulos. It lay unnoticed for years until Manuel Bartolomé Cossío found it in the Oñate Palace in Madrid.
  • It belonged to the Countess of Añover y Castañeda and then to the New York collector Mrs. H. O. Havemeyer, whose bequest brought it to the Met in 1929. Its sky is counted among the best-known in Western art, alongside that of Van Gogh’s The Starry Night.
