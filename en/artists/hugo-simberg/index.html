Hugo Simberg: biography, paintings and prints

Hamina (Finlandia), 1873 – Ähtäri, 1917

Finnish painter and printmaker, one of the leading figures of Nordic Symbolism. He studied drawing in Vyborg and Helsinki, where Helene Schjerfbeck was among his teachers, and between 1895 and 1897 he was the private pupil of Akseli Gallen-Kallela at his wilderness studio in Ruovesi. He painted melancholy, fantastical scenes with little devils, angels and a kindly figure of Death, as in The Garden of Death. Between 1904 and 1906 he decorated St John’s Church in Tampere, now the cathedral, with Magnus Enckell.
