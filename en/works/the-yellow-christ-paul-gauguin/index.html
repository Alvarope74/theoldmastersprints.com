The Yellow Christ by Paul Gauguin: meaning, size and where to see it

The Yellow Christ is an oil on canvas by Paul Gauguin, painted in 1889. It measures 92.1 × 73.3 cm and is held at the Buffalo AKG Art Museum, Buffalo.

What it shows

A crucified Christ of intense yellow fills the foreground, set in an autumn Breton landscape of ochre fields, reddish trees and gentle hills. At the foot of the cross, three Breton peasant women in white coifs and dark dresses sit or kneel in prayer, while in the distance a man climbs over a stone wall and other figures walk through the fields.

Gauguin painted it in Pont-Aven, Brittany, in the autumn of 1889, using as his model the painted wooden crucifix in the chapel of Trémalo, near the village. The dark outlines and broad areas of flat, unshaded colour are the clearest example of Synthetism: the yellow of Christ’s body is echoed in the fields, as if the religious scene and the landscape were a single vision of the peasants’ simple faith.

Did you know?

  • The wooden crucifix that served as his model is still in the chapel of Trémalo, near Pont-Aven.
  • Gauguin painted the picture again, reversed as in a mirror, in the background of his Self-Portrait with the Yellow Christ (1890–1891), now in the Musée d’Orsay.
  • The same year he painted a companion piece, The Green Christ (or Breton Calvary), now in the Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium in Brussels.

Where to see the original

It is in the Buffalo AKG Art Museum (formerly the Albright-Knox Art Gallery) in Buffalo, New York, which bought it in 1946. It measures 92 × 73 cm.
