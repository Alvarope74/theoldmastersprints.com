Rogier van der Weyden: biography, paintings and prints

Tournai, 1399/1400 – Bruselas, 1464

Early Netherlandish painter, born in Tournai as Roger de la Pasture. He trained in the workshop of Robert Campin and became a master in 1432. Around 1435 he settled in Brussels, where he took the Flemish form of his name and was appointed the city's official painter, a post created for him. He painted mainly triptychs, altarpieces and portraits, for the Dukes of Burgundy, their court and patrons in Italy and Spain, such as King John II of Castile, who gave his Miraflores Altarpiece to the Charterhouse near Burgos. In his lifetime he eclipsed Jan van Eyck in fame, thanks to the emotion of his figures and his rich colour. Almost forgotten by the 18th century, he is now counted, with Campin and Van Eyck, among the three great Netherlandish masters of the 15th century.
