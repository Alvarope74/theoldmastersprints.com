Reply of the Zaporozhian Cossacks by Ilya Repin: meaning, location and key facts

Reply of the Zaporozhian Cossacks is an oil on canvas by Ilya Repin, painted between 1880 and 1891. It measures 203 × 358 cm and is held at the Russian Museum, Saint Petersburg.

Data sourceWikidata (Q478271) · Wikipedia

What it shows

A crowd of Zaporozhian Cossacks gathers round a table where a scribe is writing their answer to the Ottoman sultan Mehmed IV, who had demanded their submission. According to the legend, set in 1676, the Cossacks under their leader Ivan Sirko replied with a letter full of insults and profanities, and Repin paints the moment when each of them comes up with a coarser jibe than the last.

The painting is a study of laughter: guffaws, mocking grins, gestures and exchanged glances across dozens of different faces. Repin worked with the historian Dmytro Yavornytsky and made studies in Yekaterinoslav (today Dnipro) and in Pashkovskaya, near Krasnodar, so that the weapons, clothes and hairstyles would be true to the period.

Did you know?

  • Repin worked on it for more than ten years, from 1880 to 1891, and recorded those years along the lower edge of the canvas.
  • Tsar Alexander III bought it for 35,000 roubles.
  • There is a second, unfinished version, begun by Repin in 1889, now in the Kharkiv Art Museum in Ukraine.

Where to see the original

It hangs in the Russian Museum in Saint Petersburg, the city's great museum of Russian art, housed in the Mikhailovsky Palace. It measures 2.03 × 3.58 metres.
