Henri Rousseau: biography, paintings and prints

Laval, 1844 – París, 1910

Self-taught French painter, nicknamed "Le Douanier" (the customs officer) because he worked for years as a toll collector at the gates of Paris. He took up painting seriously in his forties and from 1886 exhibited regularly at the Salon des Indépendants, where critics often mocked his naïve style. In 1893 he left his job to devote himself fully to painting. He is famous for his lush jungles full of wild beasts and exotic plants, although he never left France: he drew on the Jardin des Plantes and illustrated books. Admired by the avant-garde, he was honoured by Picasso in 1908 with a famous banquet at his Bateau-Lavoir studio. He is now regarded as the great master of naïve art and a forerunner of Surrealism.
