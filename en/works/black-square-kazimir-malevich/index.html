Black Square by Kazimir Malevich: meaning, location and key facts

Black Square (1915) is an oil on canvas by Kazimir Malevich, painted in 1915. It measures 79.5 × 79.5 cm and is held at the Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow.

StyleSuprematism
Data sourceWikidata (Q1170315) · Wikipedia

What it shows

A black square on a white ground. Malevich presented it in 1915 at the “0.10” exhibition in Petrograd, hung high in the corner of the room, the place where Russian homes kept their religious icons.

It is the starting point of Suprematism, the “supremacy of pure feeling” in art, which gave up depicting objects. For Malevich it was not a void but the “zero of form”.

Did you know?

  • In 2015 the Tretyakov Gallery found two earlier compositions beneath the black, and a pencil inscription alluding to a joke about a battle of black people in a dark cave.
  • Malevich painted several versions; the 1915 one is the most cracked due to the ageing of the paint.
  • It measures 79.5 × 79.5 cm.

Where to see the original

Black Square is at the Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
