Karl Bryullov: biography, paintings and prints

San Petersburgo, 1799 – Manziana (Roma), 1852

Karl Pavlovich Bryullov, born Brüllo, a Russian painter from a family of Huguenot descent; his father was an academician, woodcarver and engraver. He studied at the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg, leaving in 1821 with the great gold medal, and in 1823 settled in Rome with his brother, the architect Alexander Bryullov. In Italy he worked as a portraitist and genre painter until The Last Day of Pompeii brought him European fame. He returned in triumph to Russia, where he taught at the Academy from 1836 to 1848. While working on the ceiling of St Isaac's Cathedral his health failed, and in 1849 he left Russia on his doctors' advice, first for Madeira and then for Italy. He died in Manziana, near Rome, and is buried in the Protestant Cemetery in Rome.
