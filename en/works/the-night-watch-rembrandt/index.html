The Night Watch by Rembrandt: meaning, location and key facts

The Night Watch is an oil on canvas by Rembrandt, painted in 1642. It measures 363 × 437 cm and is held at the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam.

What it shows

Its real title is “The Company of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and Lieutenant Willem van Ruytenburch”. It is a group portrait of an Amsterdam civic militia commissioned for its headquarters, but Rembrandt turned it into a scene in motion: the captain gives the order to march, muskets are loaded and a drum rolls.

Among the militiamen stands a brightly lit girl with a chicken hanging from her belt, the company’s emblem. The nickname “Night Watch” arose in the 18th century, when darkened varnish made the scene look nocturnal; it actually takes place by day.

Did you know?

  • In 1715 it was trimmed on all four sides, especially the left, to fit a wall in Amsterdam’s town hall.
  • It has been attacked several times, with a knife in 1975 and with acid in 1990, and was restored on both occasions.
  • Since 2019 the Rijksmuseum has been studying and restoring it in public view as “Operation Night Watch”, publishing ultra-high-resolution images online.

Where to see the original

The Night Watch is at the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
