The Starry Night by Vincent van Gogh: meaning, location and key facts

The Starry Night is an oil on canvas by Vincent van Gogh, painted in 1889. It measures 73.7 × 92.1 cm and is held at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York.

What it shows

A swirling night sky with eleven stars, a crescent moon and one very bright star (identified by astronomers as Venus) hangs over a sleeping village below the hills. In the foreground a dark cypress rises like a flame, linking earth and sky.

Van Gogh painted it in June 1889 at the Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, where he had admitted himself. The view starts from his bedroom window, but the village and church are partly invented: he painted during the day, from memory.

Did you know?

  • In the year he spent at Saint-Rémy he painted around 150 canvases, including his irises and olive groves.
  • The Museum of Modern Art in New York acquired it in 1941 through the bequest of Lillie P. Bliss, one of its founders.
  • It is a mid-sized canvas, 73.7 × 92.1 cm; the thick, spiralling brushstrokes are best appreciated in large reproductions.

Where to see the original

The Starry Night is at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
