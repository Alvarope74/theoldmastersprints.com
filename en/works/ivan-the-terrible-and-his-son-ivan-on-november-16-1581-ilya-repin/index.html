Ivan the Terrible and his son Ivan on November 16, 1581 by Ilya Repin: meaning, location and key facts

Ivan the Terrible and his son Ivan on November 16, 1581 is an oil on canvas by Ilya Repin, painted between 1883 and 1885. It measures 199.5 × 254 cm and is held at the Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow.

Data sourceWikidata (Q4195595) · Wikipedia

What it shows

Tsar Ivan IV holds his dying son, the Tsarevich Ivan, whom he has just struck on the head with his staff in a fit of rage on 16 November 1581. The father’s wild stare contrasts with the calm of the son as his life ebbs away.

Repin painted it between 1883 and 1885. The red carpet, the staff on the floor and the blood running between the tsar’s fingers heighten the horror of the scene.

Did you know?

  • Its exhibition was banned for a time in 1885 on the orders of Alexander III.
  • It was attacked with a knife in 1913 and with a metal post in 2018, and was restored both times.
  • The painter Grigory Myasoyedov posed as the tsar and the writer Vsevolod Garshin as his son.
