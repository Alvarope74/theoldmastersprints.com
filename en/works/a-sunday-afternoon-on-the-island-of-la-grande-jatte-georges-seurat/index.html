A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte by Georges Seurat: meaning, location and key facts

A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte is an oil on canvas by Georges Seurat, painted between 1884 and 1886. It measures 207.5 × 308.1 cm and is held at the Art Institute of Chicago.

What it shows

Parisians of different classes spend a Sunday on the island of La Grande Jatte in the Seine: strollers, a woman with a monkey on a leash, a girl in white, boatmen and rowers. The figures look still and geometric, almost like statues.

Seurat painted it between 1884 and 1886 with small dots of pure colour that the eye blends at a distance, the technique known as pointillism or divisionism. It was shown at the eighth and last Impressionist exhibition in 1886.

Did you know?

  • It is more than two by three metres and took him two years, with dozens of preparatory studies.
  • Seurat later added a painted border of dots in complementary colours.
  • The collector Frederic Clay Bartlett bought it in 1924 and gave it to the Art Institute of Chicago in 1926.
