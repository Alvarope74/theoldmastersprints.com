The Milkmaid by Johannes Vermeer: meaning, location and key facts

The Milkmaid is an oil on canvas by Johannes Vermeer, painted between about 1658 and 1660. It measures 45.5 × 41 cm and is held at the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam.

Key facts

ArtistJohannes Vermeer
Datec. 1658–1660
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions45.5 × 41 cm (height × width)
MuseumRijksmuseum, Amsterdam
StyleDutch Golden Age
Data sourceWikidata (Q167605) · Wikipedia

What it shows

A maid pours milk from a jug into a bowl, absorbed in her task, in a kitchen lit by a window on the left. On the table are bread, a basket and a blue jug; on the bare wall, a nail and its shadow.

Vermeer turns a domestic gesture into an image of almost solemn calm. The bread is painted with tiny dots of light and the apron uses ultramarine blue. On the floor at the right are a foot warmer and a strip of Delft tiles.

Did you know?

  • It measures just 45.5 × 41 cm.
  • The Rijksmuseum acquired it in 1908.
  • X-rays show that Vermeer painted and then removed other objects, such as a basket next to the foot warmer.

Where to see the original

The Milkmaid is at the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
