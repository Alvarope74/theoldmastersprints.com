The Anatomy Lesson of Dr. Nicolaes Tulp by Rembrandt: meaning, location and key facts

The Anatomy Lesson of Dr. Nicolaes Tulp is an oil on canvas by Rembrandt, painted in 1632. It measures 169.5 × 216.5 cm and is held at the Mauritshuis, The Hague.

What it shows

Dr Nicolaes Tulp explains the anatomy of the forearm to seven members of the Amsterdam surgeons’ guild. With forceps he lifts the flexor muscles, and with his other hand he mimics the gesture those muscles make possible.

It was the guild’s annual public dissection, in January 1632, on the body of Aris Kindt, an executed thief. Rembrandt was 26 and this commission established him as the city’s leading portraitist.

Did you know?

  • Instead of lining up the sitters, Rembrandt groups them in a pyramid of gazes converging on the dissected arm.
  • The sheet held by one of the surgeons lists the names of those present, added later.
  • King William I of the Netherlands bought it in 1828 for the Mauritshuis.
