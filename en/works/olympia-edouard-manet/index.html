Olympia by Édouard Manet: meaning, location and key facts

Olympia is an oil on canvas by Édouard Manet, painted in 1863. It measures 130.5 × 191 cm and is held at the Musée d'Orsay, Paris.

What it shows

A naked woman reclines on a bed and looks straight at the viewer without modesty. She wears a black ribbon around her neck, a bracelet and a flower in her hair. Her maid brings her a bouquet, probably from a client, and at her feet a black cat arches its back.

Manet reworked Titian’s Venus of Urbino: the sleeping dog of the original, a symbol of fidelity, becomes a cat. Olympia was a common name among Parisian prostitutes, and the public immediately understood who she was.

Did you know?

  • It was painted in 1863 but shown at the Salon of 1865, where it had to be guarded to protect it from attacks.
  • The model was Victorine Meurent, the same as in Luncheon on the Grass.
  • In 1890 Claude Monet organised a subscription among friends to buy it and donate it to the French state.

Where to see the original

Olympia is at the Musée d'Orsay, Paris. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
