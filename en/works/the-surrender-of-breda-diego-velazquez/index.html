The Surrender of Breda by Diego Velázquez: meaning, location and key facts

The Surrender of Breda is an oil on canvas by Diego Velázquez, painted between 1634 and 1635. It measures 307 × 367 cm and is held at the Museo del Prado, Madrid.

What it shows

On 5 June 1625, after a long siege, the Dutch governor Justin of Nassau hands the keys of Breda to General Ambrogio Spinola, commander of the Spanish army. Spinola places a hand on his shoulder and stops him from kneeling: a scene of generosity towards the defeated.

On the right, the vertical pikes of the Spanish troops form a forest that gives the painting its other name, The Lances. In the background are the smoke of fires and the floodwaters of the siege.

Did you know?

  • Velázquez painted it in 1634-1635 for the Hall of Realms in the Buen Retiro palace, alongside other victories of Philip IV’s reign.
  • Velázquez never went to Breda; he relied on prints and written accounts.
  • The man in a hat at the far right is often identified as a self-portrait.
