The Ambassadors by Hans Holbein the Younger: meaning, location and key facts

The Ambassadors is an oil on oak panel by Hans Holbein the Younger, painted in 1533. It measures 207 × 209 cm and is held at the National Gallery, London.

Key facts

ArtistHans Holbein the Younger
Date1533
MediumOil on oak panel
Dimensions207 × 209 cm (height × width)
MuseumNational Gallery, London
StyleRenaissance
Data sourceWikidata (Q1212937) · Wikipedia

What it shows

Two French diplomats in London, Jean de Dinteville and Georges de Selve, stand life-size beside shelves full of scientific instruments, a globe, books, a lute with a broken string and a Lutheran hymnbook. Everything alludes to learning and to the religious tensions of 1533.

On the floor, a strange elongated shape is in fact a skull distorted in anamorphosis: it can only be recognised by looking at the painting from the side, from the right and very close. It is a reminder of death in the midst of luxury.

Did you know?

  • At the top left, half hidden by the curtain, is a small crucifix.
  • The National Gallery in London bought it in 1890.
  • It measures 2.07 × 2.09 metres, almost square.

Where to see the original

The Ambassadors is at the National Gallery, London. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
