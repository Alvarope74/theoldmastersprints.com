Sistine Madonna by Raphael: meaning, location and key facts

Sistine Madonna is an oil on canvas by Raphael, painted around 1512. It measures 269.5 × 201 cm and is held at the Gemäldegalerie Alte Meister, Dresden.

What it shows

Two green curtains open as if on a stage to reveal the Virgin holding the Christ Child, stepping barefoot towards us on the clouds. On the left, Pope Saint Sixtus, his tiara resting on the ledge below, points out of the picture; on the right, Saint Barbara lowers her gaze. The background, which looks like a golden sky, is made up of dozens of faintly sketched angels' heads.

Raphael painted it on commission from Pope Julius II for the high altar of the church of San Sisto in Piacenza. Below, leaning on the ledge, two small winged cherubs gaze upwards looking bored or dreamy; they have become one of the most reproduced images in art history, on postcards, stamps and all kinds of objects.

Did you know?

  • In 1754 Augustus III of Poland, Elector of Saxony, bought it from the monks of San Sisto, and it has been in Dresden ever since.
  • At the end of the Second World War Soviet troops took it to Moscow; it returned to Dresden in 1955.
  • It is one of the last Madonnas that Raphael painted entirely with his own hand.

Where to see the original

It hangs in the Gemäldegalerie Alte Meister in Dresden, the Old Masters picture gallery housed in the Zwinger palace. It measures 269.5 × 201 cm.
