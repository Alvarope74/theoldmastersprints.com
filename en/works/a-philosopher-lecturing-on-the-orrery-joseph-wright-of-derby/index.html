A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery by Joseph Wright of Derby: meaning, size and where to see it

A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery is an oil on canvas by Joseph Wright of Derby, painted in 1766. It measures 147.2 × 203.2 cm and is held at the Derby Museum and Art Gallery.

What it shows

In a darkened room, a group gathers around an orrery, a mechanical model of the Solar System with large brass rings. Standing behind it, a grey-haired scholar in a red robe explains how it works; on the left, a man takes notes and, beside him, a young woman gazes at the device. Two children lean over the rings in fascination, a boy seen from behind blocks the light with his silhouette, and on the right a young man rests his head on his hand, lost in thought, next to a seated man.

The only light comes from a lamp placed at the centre in place of the Sun, hidden behind the boy seen from behind: hence the full title, A Philosopher giving that Lecture on the Orrery in which a lamp is put in place of the Sun. The shadows cast by the lamp were part of the demonstration. Wright treated a scientific subject with the solemnity previously reserved for religious scenes: to him, the marvels of science were as awe-inspiring as a miracle.

Did you know?

  • Wright painted it without a commission, probably with Washington Shirley, 5th Earl Ferrers, in mind, a naval officer who owned an orrery of his own. Ferrers bought it for £210 after it was shown at the Society of Artists in 1766; he is thought to appear seated beside the orrery, and the man taking notes is thought to be Wright’s friend Peter Perez Burdett.
  • Look closely at the adults’ faces and each one shows one of the main phases of the Moon, new moon, half moon, gibbous moon and full moon, depending on how the lamplight falls on them.
  • It was the second of his great candlelit paintings, after Three Persons Viewing the Gladiator by Candlelight (1765), and came before An Experiment on a Bird in the Air Pump (1768), now in the National Gallery in London.

Where to see the original

It is in the Derby Museum and Art Gallery, in the painter’s home town, on permanent display beside a working replica of the grand orrery in the painting, which the museum commissioned from the clock and orrery maker John Gleave in 1993. It measures 147.2 × 203.2 cm.
