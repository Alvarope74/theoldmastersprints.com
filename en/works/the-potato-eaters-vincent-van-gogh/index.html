The Potato Eaters by Vincent van Gogh: meaning, location and key facts

The Potato Eaters is an oil on canvas by Vincent van Gogh, painted in 1885. It measures 82 × 114 cm and is held at the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam.

What it shows

Five peasants share a supper of potatoes and coffee by the light of an oil lamp in a cottage in Nuenen, the Brabant village where Van Gogh’s family lived. The colours are earthy and dark, and hands and faces are painted with deliberate roughness.

Van Gogh wanted to show that these people “have tilled the earth themselves with these hands they are putting in the dish”, honestly earning their food. He painted it in April 1885, after months of studies of peasants’ heads and hands.

Did you know?

  • Van Gogh considered it his first major work, painted five years before his death and before his palette brightened in Paris.
  • A preliminary version is in the Kröller-Müller Museum, and there is a lithograph with the scene reversed.
  • His friend the painter Anthon van Rappard harshly criticised the lithograph, and their friendship broke down.

Where to see the original

The Potato Eaters is at the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
