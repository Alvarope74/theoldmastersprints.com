Liberty Leading the People by Eugène Delacroix: meaning, location and key facts

Liberty Leading the People is an oil on canvas by Eugène Delacroix, painted in 1830. It measures 260 × 325 cm and is held at the Louvre, Paris.

Key facts

ArtistEugène Delacroix
Date1830
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions260 × 325 cm (height × width)
MuseumLouvre, Paris
StyleRomanticism
Data sourceWikidata (Q29530) · Wikipedia

What it shows

A woman in a Phrygian cap, the allegory of Liberty, strides over a barricade holding the French tricolour in one hand and a bayonetted musket in the other. She is followed by a bourgeois in a top hat, a worker with a sabre and a boy brandishing two pistols, while the fallen lie at her feet.

Delacroix painted the scene in autumn 1830, a few months after the Three Glorious Days, the July uprising that overthrew Charles X. In the smoke behind, the towers of Notre-Dame place the fight in the real Paris.

Did you know?

  • The French state bought it in 1831 but kept it out of public view for years because of its revolutionary charge.
  • The boy with the pistols is often said to have inspired Victor Hugo’s Gavroche in Les Misérables (1862).
  • Between 2023 and 2024 the Louvre restored it, removing yellowed varnish that had dulled the blue, white and red of the flag.

Where to see the original

Liberty Leading the People is at the Louvre, Paris. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
