Where Do We Come From? What Are We? Where Are We Going? by Paul Gauguin: meaning, size and where to see it

Where Do We Come From? What Are We? Where Are We Going? is an oil on canvas by Paul Gauguin, painted between 1897 and 1898. It measures 139.1 × 374.6 cm and is held at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.

What it shows

A great frieze of Tahitian figures in a landscape of trees, water and blue mountains, meant to be read from right to left. On the right, a sleeping baby beside three women stands for the beginning of life; in the centre, a young man reaches up to pick a fruit, adulthood; on the left, a crouching old woman, close to death, sits beside a strange white bird. Behind them, a blue idol with raised arms stands for what Gauguin called “the Beyond”.

Gauguin painted it in Tahiti at the end of 1897, at a time of illness, debt and grief over the death of his daughter Aline, and he conceived it as the great synthesis of his work. The flat colours, the yellow upper corners, like a fresco with gilded corners, and the absence of a single scene make the painting a meditation on the questions of its title rather than a story.

Did you know?

  • Gauguin wrote the title himself in French in the upper left corner: “D’où venons nous / Que sommes nous / Où allons nous”.
  • In his letters to his friend Daniel de Monfreid, Gauguin said that after finishing it he tried to kill himself with arsenic.
  • It was first shown in 1898 at Ambroise Vollard’s gallery in Paris, and the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston bought it in 1936.

Where to see the original

It is in the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, one of the great museums of the United States, on Huntington Avenue. It measures 139.1 × 374.6 cm, one of the largest paintings Gauguin ever made.
