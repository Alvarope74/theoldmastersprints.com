What it shows

Dürer painted himself early in 1500, shortly before his 29th birthday: it is the last of his three painted self-portraits. Unlike the earlier ones, where he showed off fashionable clothes and hair in the usual three-quarter view, here he faces us head-on, symmetrical, against a black background with no landscape or window. In 1500 such a frontal pose was associated with medieval religious art, above all with images of Christ as Salvator Mundi, with a short beard, moustache and parted brown hair.

He wears a Schaube, the long coat of well-to-do burghers, with a wide fur collar that his hand touches with slender fingers; every hair of the fur is painted with its own brushstroke. On either side of the face, as if floating in the dark, are the date 1500 above his AD monogram, which can thus also be read as Anno Domini, and a Latin inscription: “I, Albrecht Dürer of Nuremberg, portrayed myself in my own colours at the age of 28.”

Did you know?

  • It is the only Dürer self-portrait in which his painting hand does not appear: the other hand is outside the picture, and it is unclear whether the hand we see is his reflection in the mirror or his right hand.
  • Dürer, who painted his hair reddish-blond in other self-portraits, darkened it here. The face looked so much like an image of Christ that around 1520 Sebald Beham copied it for a Christ in a woodcut, which was accepted as a Dürer until the 19th century.
  • It was probably on public display in Nuremberg from shortly before the painter’s death in 1528 until 1805, when it was sold to the Bavarian royal collection.
