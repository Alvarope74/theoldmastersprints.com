Caspar David Friedrich: biography, paintings and prints

Greifswald (Pomerania sueca), 1774 – Dresde, 1840

German landscape painter, the leading figure of Romanticism in his country. The son of a candle-maker and soap boiler, he lost his mother as a child and saw his younger brother drown in a frozen lake. He studied at the Academy of Copenhagen from 1794 to 1798 and then settled in Dresden. His landscapes, composed in the studio from sketches made outdoors, set contemplative figures against night skies, mists, barren trees or Gothic ruins. He was elected a member of the Berlin Academy in 1810. He fell out of fashion in his later years and died in obscurity; an exhibition in Berlin in 1906 began his rediscovery.
