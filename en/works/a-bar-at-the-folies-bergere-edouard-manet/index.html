A Bar at the Folies-Bergère by Édouard Manet: meaning, location and key facts

A Bar at the Folies-Bergère is an oil on canvas by Édouard Manet, painted in 1882. It measures 96 × 130 cm and is held at the Courtauld Gallery, London.

What it shows

A barmaid stands behind the marble counter of the Folies-Bergère, the famous Paris music hall, looking out at us with a distant expression. In front of her are bottles of champagne and beer, a glass bowl of mandarins and two flowers in a glass. Behind her, a huge mirror reflects the crowded hall, the chandeliers and, in the upper left corner, the green-booted feet of a trapeze artist.

The mirror is the puzzle of the painting: the barmaid's reflection is shifted to the right, leaning towards a man in a top hat who, in a real scene, would block our view. Manet did not paint an exact scene but an image of modern Parisian life in which the central figure seems alone in the middle of the crowd.

Did you know?

  • The barmaid was a real person, known as Suzon, who worked at the Folies-Bergère in the early 1880s.
  • The beer bottles can be identified by the red triangle on the label as Bass Pale Ale, an English beer.
  • It was shown at the Paris Salon of 1882 and is considered Manet's last major work. Its first owner was the composer Emmanuel Chabrier, a close friend of the painter, who hung it over his piano.

Where to see the original

It hangs in the Courtauld Gallery in London, in Somerset House on the Strand. It measures 96 × 130 cm.
