The Sleeping Gypsy by Henri Rousseau: meaning, size and where to see it

The Sleeping Gypsy is an oil on canvas by Henri Rousseau, painted in 1897. It measures 51 × 79 cm and is held at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York.

What it shows

In an arid desert, under a night sky with a few stars and a full moon, a dark-skinned woman lies asleep on a striped blanket. She wears a long robe with coloured stripes, holds a staff in her right hand and has a mandolin and a tall, slim-necked jar beside her. A maned lion has come up behind her, lowers its head and sniffs her; the moonlight catches its mane. In the background, low mountains and a river close the horizon.

Rousseau described the scene himself: a wandering mandolin player sleeps with her jar of drinking water beside her, and a lion that happens to pass picks up her scent but does not devour her; there is, he added, a moonlight effect, "very poetic". Painted without shadows and in flat, even colours, the picture is a prime example of Rousseau's naïve art, and may draw on his visits to the animals of the Jardin des Plantes in Paris and the reconstructed colonial villages of the 1889 World's Fair.

Did you know?

  • It was first shown at the Salon des Indépendants in 1897. Rousseau then tried, without success, to sell it to the mayor of Laval, his home town.
  • It entered the collection of a Parisian charcoal merchant, where it stayed until 1924. That year the critic Louis Vauxcelles discovered it, the dealer Daniel-Henry Kahnweiler bought it, a controversy arose over whether it was a forgery, and the American collector John Quinn acquired it.
  • After Quinn's death it was sold at the Hôtel Drouot in Paris and passed through a Swiss collection. In 1939 Olga Guggenheim, wife of Simon Guggenheim, bought it and gave it to MoMA, where it hangs next to Van Gogh's The Starry Night.

It is in the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York. It measures 129.5 × 200.7 cm.
