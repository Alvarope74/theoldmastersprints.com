Las Meninas by Diego Velázquez: meaning, location and key facts

Las Meninas is an oil on canvas by Diego Velázquez, painted in 1656. It measures 318 × 276 cm and is held at the Museo del Prado, Madrid.

Data sourceWikidata (Q208758) · Wikipedia

What it shows

The Infanta Margarita, daughter of Philip IV, stands in the centre surrounded by her ladies-in-waiting (meninas) María Agustina Sarmiento and Isabel de Velasco, the dwarfs Mari Bárbola and Nicolasito Pertusato and a mastiff. On the left, Velázquez portrays himself with palette and brush in front of a huge canvas whose back is all we see.

The mirror at the back reflects King Philip IV and Queen Mariana of Austria, who would be standing where the viewer is. In the doorway, against the light, stands the palace chamberlain José Nieto. The scene takes place in Madrid’s Alcázar palace in 1656.

Did you know?

  • The red cross of the Order of Santiago on Velázquez’s chest was added after 1659, when he was admitted to the order.
  • Royal inventories called it “The Family of Philip IV”; the title Las Meninas appears in the Prado catalogue of 1843.
  • Picasso painted 58 interpretations of it in 1957, now in the Museu Picasso in Barcelona.

Where to see the original

Las Meninas is at the Museo del Prado, Madrid. Works are sometimes lent to exhibitions or taken down for restoration, so it is worth checking the museum’s website before you go.
